# Declaration

**Universal Modular Generation (UMG)** is a novel, technology-agnostic paradigm for explicitly specifying artificial cognition as a structured, modular, and inspectable artifact prior to execution.

UMG was developed to address a persistent limitation in intelligent system design: while modern systems exhibit increasingly sophisticated behavior, the cognition that governs that behavior is typically implicit, opaque, and distributed across prompts, agent logic, heuristics, and runtime control flow. UMG formalizes cognition itself as a first-class design concern, rather than treating it as an emergent side effect of execution.

UMG operates within a **Cognitive Specification Layer (CSL)**, a novel abstraction boundary that identifies a previously under-formalized jurisdiction in artificial systems: the pre-runtime specification and resolution of cognition. CSL sits below human cognition and above model execution. It does not define how intelligence works and does not perform execution. Instead, it defines *where* artificial cognition is specified, *how* authority over reasoning is resolved, and *when* systems are permitted to act.

Within CSL, intent, constraints, priorities, values, and structural directives are represented explicitly and resolved deterministically. These specifications are compiled into cognitive artifacts that constrain and guide downstream intelligent executors while remaining non-executing themselves. This separation establishes cognition as an explicit, inspectable artifact rather than an implicit property of prompts or runtime behavior.

UMG does not replace models, agents, or execution frameworks. It is model-agnostic, runtime-agnostic, and implementation-independent. Its contribution is the identification and formalization of a missing layer in intelligent system design, enabling deterministic resolution, cross-model portability, auditability, and governance of cognitive behavior without retraining, prompt entanglement, or runtime coupling.

In this sense, **UMG introduces a new paradigm for designing cognition**, and **CSL names the abstraction boundary that paradigm makes explicit**. The novelty lies not in new algorithms or execution mechanisms, but in isolating and formalizing cognition as a governed, pre-runtime artifact within artificial systems.
